# AP Biology / AP European History / AP Chemistry: what must be memorized, and how elementary kids can start

Question (translated from Chinese): Do AP Biology, AP European History and AP Chemistry contain must-memorize knowledge? Pair each with a format that lets elementary-school kids (grades ~3-6) start learning it.

## 0. Sources, citation convention, and caveats

All claims come from College Board primary sources. The CED PDFs were downloaded and the text was searched locally.

| Course | CED (primary) | Official exam page (primary) |
|---|---|---|
| AP Biology | https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-biology-course-and-exam-description.pdf (footer "2025 College Board", V.1) | https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-biology/exam |
| AP European History | https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-european-history-course-and-exam-description.pdf (footer "2026 College Board", V.1) | https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-european-history/exam |
| AP Chemistry | https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-chemistry-course-and-exam-description.pdf (footer "2024 College Board", V.1) | https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-chemistry/exam |

- Page citation: "CED p.N" is the page number printed in the CED footer. The PDF index is given as "(pdf N)". The printed number is the PDF index minus 7 for Bio and Chem, and minus 8 for Euro History.
- Nothing here is "secondary, unverified" unless it is marked that way. Items I could not tie to a CED page are labelled **[not in CED]**. Those are my own kid-curriculum picks, not College Board requirements.
- The official exam pages say nothing about memorization. Their wording on reference materials: Bio and Chem say reference material is printed, mailed to schools and in Bluebook. Euro History says nothing about it. The CED is the authority on exam content.
- Exam dates differ by page (Chem May 6, 2027; Euro May 4, 2027). They are irrelevant here.

### 1.1 AP Biology

**Units and multiple-choice weighting** (CED p.17 (pdf 24); each unit opener repeats it, e.g. p.29 (pdf 36)). The table in the PDF is garbled by text extraction, so the percentages below were read from the individual unit-opener pages.

| Unit | Weighting |
|---|---|
| 1 Chemistry of Life | 8-11% |
| 2 Cells | 10-13% |
| 3 Cellular Energetics | 12-16% |
| 4 Cell Communication and Cell Cycle | 10-15% |
| 5 Heredity | 8-11% |
| 6 Gene Expression and Regulation | 12-16% |
| 7 Natural Selection | 13-20% |
| 8 Ecology | 10-15% |

Exam: 3 hours, 60 multiple-choice questions (90 min, 50%) and 6 free-response questions (90 min, 50%). A 4-function, scientific or graphing calculator is allowed. (CED p.197 (pdf 204); https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-biology/exam)

**What College Board says students must KNOW**
- "Essential knowledge statements define the required content knowledge associated with each learning objective assessed on the AP Exam." (CED p.25 (pdf 32))
- **Exclusion statements** mark what is NOT assessed: "The content addressed in the exclusion statements will not be assessed on the AP Biology Exam." (CED p.25 (pdf 32))
- Illustrative examples are "suggested, not required" (same page).
- Memorization is explicitly de-emphasized:
  - "It is important to make clear the distinction between memorizing details and demonstrating an integrated understanding..." (CED p.178 (pdf 185), Science Practice 1)
  - The Unit 3 teacher note says the same about molecules (CED p.65 (pdf 72)).

**What is PROVIDED on the exam**
- The AP Biology Equations and Formulas sheet (CED p.230 (pdf 237)) gives:
  - mean, standard deviation, standard error, chi-square with the critical-value table, and laws of probability;
  - Hardy-Weinberg (p2 + 2pq + q2 = 1, p + q = 1);
  - mode/median/range definitions and metric prefixes.
- Topic pages also give geometry equations (volume and surface area of sphere, cube and cylinder; CED p.52 (pdf 59)).
- The official exam page says reference information is available in Bluebook and printed for schools.

**Skills-based**
- Six science practices are assessed on every exam: Concept Explanation, Visual Representations, Questions and Methods, Data Analysis, Mathematical Routines, Argumentation. (CED p.~198-199, "How Student Learning Is Assessed")
- Section II is 2 long and 4 short free-response questions on experimental design, data, models, graphing and conceptual analysis. (CED p.197 (pdf 204))

**Is there must-memorize content? Yes, a moderate vocabulary and structure core. Mechanisms, data and experiments dominate.**
- The "required content" is essential-knowledge statements that name structures, molecules and stages. The student must recall these words to answer reasoning questions.
- The CED explicitly excludes deep memorization: Calvin-cycle steps, enzyme names (except ATP synthase), specific cyclin/CdK pairs, structures of specific carbohydrates, nucleotides and amino acids.
- Many "facts" are relationships, not lists. Examples are structure-to-function, inputs and outputs of pathways, and predicting the effect of a change.

**Concrete memorizable items tied to the CED**

| Item | CED location |
|---|---|
| Elements of life: C, H, O dominant; S in proteins; P in phospholipids and nucleic acids; N in nucleic acids | Topic 1.2, p.33 (pdf 40) |
| Macromolecule/monomer pairs (carbohydrate/monosaccharide, protein/amino acid, nucleic acid/nucleotide; lipids) and examples: cellulose, starch, glycogen | Topics 1.3-1.5, p.35 (pdf 42), p.37 (pdf 44) |
| Lipid types: fats, steroids (incl. cholesterol), phospholipids, and their functions | Topic 1.5, p.37 (pdf 44) |
| Nucleotide parts: 5-carbon sugar (deoxyribose/ribose), phosphate, base (adenine, thymine, guanine, cytosine, uracil) | Topic 1.6, p.38 (pdf 45) |
| Amino acid parts: central carbon, H, carboxyl, amine, R group; R-group types hydrophobic/polar/ionic | Topic 1.7, p.40 (pdf 47) |
| Organelles and functions: ribosome, ER (rough/smooth), Golgi, lysosome, vacuole, mitochondrion, chloroplast, nuclear envelope, plasma membrane | Topic 2.1, p.49-51 (pdf 56-58) |
| Membrane: phospholipid bilayer, fluid mosaic model | Topic 2.3, p.54 (pdf 61) |
| Cell-cycle stages: prophase, metaphase, anaphase (etc.); meiosis I/II stages, crossing over | Topics 4.5, 5.2, p.~90, p.100 (pdf 97, 107) |
| DNA replication players: helicase, topoisomerase, primer, DNA polymerase, 5' to 3' | Topic 6.2, p.113 (pdf 120) |
| Photosynthesis inputs/outputs: CO2 + H2O + light gives carbohydrate + O2; ATP synthase | Topic 3.4, p.73 (pdf 80) |
| Food chain/web and energy-flow arrows (the CED notes students often place arrows wrongly) | Unit 8 opener, pdf 156 |

**Explicitly NOT assessed (useful to avoid wasted effort)**
- Calvin-cycle steps and enzyme names, except ATP synthase (p.73).
- Molecular structure of amino acids (p.40) and of specific nucleotides (p.38).
- Structure of specific carbohydrate polymers (p.35).
- Specific cyclin-CdK pairs or growth factors (p.91).
- Sexual reproduction cycles of specific plants and animals (p.100).

Implication for the site: the existing Amino Acids Matching sets (full names to 3-letter/1-letter codes) are not required by the AP Bio CED. They are fine as general biology, but they are not AP-targeted.

**What College Board says students must KNOW**
- "Historical development statements comprise the knowledge required to demonstrate mastery of the learning objective." (CED p.32 (pdf 40))
- Each topic page lists "Illustrative Examples" (people, works, events).
  - The CED does not use "required" for them in the pages I searched. I found no sentence defining their status. Treat them as the CED's own list of recommended names (**inference**).
  - The unit pages explicitly cover seven themes: Interaction of Europe and the World; Economic and Commercial Developments; Cultural and Intellectual Developments; States and Other Institutions of Power; Social Organization and Development; National and European Identity; Technological and Scientific Innovation. (CED p.236 (pdf 244))
- Skills: contextualization, comparison, causation, continuity and change, argumentation, source analysis. The topic pages tag each topic with a reasoning process and suggested skill (e.g. Topic 1.9, p.36 (pdf 44)).

**What is PROVIDED on the exam**
- Sources: the exam supplies texts, images, graphs and maps for multiple choice and short answer, and 7 documents for the DBQ (https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-european-history/exam). Students do not have to recall the documents themselves.
- No formula or reference sheet. The official exam page is silent about reference materials (so none is promised).

**Is there must-memorize content? Yes, as background knowledge. The exam is not a recall test.**
- Questions are stimulus-based, and the DBQ and long essay require evidence. Students need outside knowledge to contextualize sources and to supply evidence in the long essay.
- The CED's named people, events and works (Illustrative Examples) are the practical "must-recognize" list.
- Dates are sparse in the CED. It uses period ranges (c.1450, 1648, 1815, 1914) more than single years.
- No single list of "key dates" is published by College Board. A canonical date list would be secondary, unverified.

**Units and weighting** (CED p.16 (pdf 23); each unit opener repeats it). The PDF table is garbled by text extraction, so the percentages below were read from the unit-opener pages.

| Unit | Weighting |
|---|---|
| 1 Atomic Structure and Properties | 7-9% |
| 2 Compound Structure and Properties | 7-9% |
| 3 Properties of Substances and Mixtures | 18-22% |
| 4 Chemical Reactions | 7-9% |
| 5 Kinetics | 7-9% |
| 6 Thermochemistry | 7-9% |
| 7 Equilibrium | 7-9% |
| 8 Acids and Bases | 11-15% |
| 9 Thermodynamics and Electrochemistry | 7-9% |

The unit-opener pages (pdf 34, 48, 62, 82, 98, 116, 132, 150, 170) give exactly one weighting each.

Exam: 3 hours 15 minutes, 60 multiple-choice questions (90 min, 50%) and 7 free-response questions (105 min, 50%): 3 long worth 10 points and 4 short worth 4 points. A scientific or graphing calculator is recommended. (CED p.205 (pdf 212); https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-chemistry/exam)

**What College Board says students must KNOW**
- Learning objectives define the content domain, and every exam question is aligned to a learning objective and a skill. (CED p.16 (pdf 23))
- Exclusion statements (e.g. pdf 30, 38, 41, 44, 54, 92) mark what is not assessed.
- **Direct memorization statement:** "Rote memorization of 'solubility rules' other than those implied in 4.7.A.5 will not be assessed on the AP Exam." (CED p.85 (pdf 92)) The implied rule is that all sodium, potassium, ammonium and nitrate salts are soluble.
- Periodic trends: the Unit 1 opener says that "while students can memorize periodic trends, they struggle to explain the electrostatic interactions within an atom that produces period trends" (CED p.22 (pdf 29)). So the exam wants explanation, not just recall.

**What is PROVIDED on the exam**
- "Students are provided with the periodic table and a formula sheet that lists specific and relevant formulas for use on the exam." (CED p.205 (pdf 212); Appendixes pp.225-229)
- "All of the equations in this course framework appear on the equation sheet provided to students." (CED p.16 (pdf 23))
- The sheet and constants cover:
  - Avogadro's number (p.30, pdf 37);
  - the kinetics, equilibrium, pH/pOH, Kw = Ka x Kb and Henderson-Hasselbalch relations;
  - thermodynamics/electrochemistry relations (Faraday's constant, p.229 (pdf 236)).

**Skills-based**
- Six science practices, including model analysis, mathematical routines, experimental design and argumentation. Every question pairs a learning objective with a skill (p.16).

**Is there must-memorize content? Yes, a small recall core, because the periodic table is provided. Most points come from reasoning and calculation.**
- Because the table and formulas are given, the genuinely memorized items are few.
- Explicit memorization (the CED lists them in essential knowledge):
  - the strong acids named in EK 8.2.A.1: HCl, HBr, HI, HClO4, H2SO4 and HNO3 (CED p.147 (pdf 154));
  - strong bases are group I and II hydroxides (same page);
  - the soluble-salt rule above;
  - typical ion charges are "predicted by their location on the periodic table" (Topic 1.8, p.38 (pdf 45)), so it is a pattern, not a memorized table.
- Not found in the CED: any list of polyatomic ions, element names or symbols, or the full solubility rules. A claim that students must know common polyatomic ions (nitrate, sulfate, etc.) is **secondary, unverified**. In the CED, ions appear only inside examples (e.g. nitrate and ammonium appear in the soluble-salt rule).
- Concepts that are "known" in the sense of reasoning:
  - periodic trends (ionization energy, atomic and ionic radii, electron affinity, electronegativity) (Topic 1.7, p.37 (pdf 44));
  - ground-state electron configuration via the Aufbau principle (Topic 1.5; exceptions are not assessed, p.37);
  - Lewis structures, formal charge and VSEPR;
  - intermolecular forces;
  - redox terms and oxidation numbers;
  - acid-base vocabulary.

## 2. Kid-friendly plan (grades ~3-6)

### 2.1 What the site can already render

Based on `index.html`, `app.js`, `data/match.json` and `data/biology.json`:

| Format | Exists today | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **SVG label-click quiz** (Learn + Quiz) | Yes (body, brain, acell, pcell, maps) | A new topic needs a new SVG in `maps/` with element ids that match a new `data/*.json`, plus a `region-card` in `index.html`. The SVG is the real cost. |
| **Grid quiz** (periodic table) | Yes (`data/elements.json`, `maps/elements.svg`) | Elements 1-118 split into 11 sets (elem-1..elem-11). |
| **Matching** (two columns, pair cards) | Yes (`startMatch` in `app.js`) | Text only. Add a set by appending 10 rows to `data/match.json` with a `match-*` region plus a `region-card` in the HTML. **No new code.** |
| **Timeline ordering** (drag/tap into order) | **No** | New code: a small view that shows shuffled cards and checks order. It is reusable by all three courses (history events, cell-cycle stages, photosynthesis steps). |
| **Flashcards with images** | **No** | New code, plus image assets. |
| **Songs/mnemonics** | **No** | Needs audio assets; the site already ships audio for Focus, so the plumbing may exist (not verified). |
| **Map click** | Yes, Geography | `europe.svg` already exists for modern Europe. The CED's historical Europe needs new maps or none. |

### 2.2 Honest scoping

- A grade 3-6 child cannot do AP-style reasoning. The realistic goal is **vocabulary, labelled diagrams, ordered sequences and "who is this person"**, i.e. the recall layer that later AP work builds on.
- Recommended order of difficulty for kids: Chemistry (symbols, table), then Biology (organelles, body systems), then European History (names and dates need context).
- Every example below is written to be recognizable to a child, with an English term and a Chinese gloss. English is the AP language, so keep the English term primary.
